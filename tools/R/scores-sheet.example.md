## What each landscape is

- `all-extensive-arable` — Every hexagon extensive arable.
- `all-intensive-arable` — Every hexagon intensive arable. Expect the worst pollution numbers.
- `all-extensive-livestock` — Every hexagon extensive livestock.
- `all-intensive-livestock` — Every hexagon intensive livestock.
- `all-agropark` — Every hexagon agropark.
- `all-nature` — Every hexagon extra nature. No agriculture at all.
- `half-intensive-half-nature` — Alternating intensive arable and nature.
- `mostly-nature-some-farms` — 46 of 465 extensive arable, the rest nature — roughly what a cautious player builds.

## Two caveats, both of which affect how you should read the table

**1. The scores are relative to each round, not to an absolute scale.** Every indicator is
normalised `(x - min) / (max - min) * 100` over that round's own surface, so a value near 50
means "about average *for this landscape*", not "moderate in absolute terms". A landscape
of uniformly mild pollution and one of uniformly severe pollution can both average near 50.
Comparing rows may therefore be less meaningful than it looks — this is the single thing most
worth a second opinion.

**2. NaN is a real result, not a crash.** It appears when an indicator's surface ends up empty
or uniform, because the normalisation above then divides by zero. `all-nature` reaches it: with
no agriculture there is no agricultural pollution anywhere, so human health has no cells left
to score. What it *should* say instead is a modelling question, which is part of what this
sheet is asking.

## Questions worth answering while you read it

These are the things the people who built the harness cannot judge:

1. **Do the five indicators move independently, or together?** This is a *tradeoff* game, so
   the interesting case is a landscape that improves one indicator and worsens another. If
   every column rises and falls as one, there is nothing for a player to trade off, and that
   would be worth knowing before a workshop rather than during one.
2. **Is the direction right?** For each indicator, does the landscape you would expect to score
   worst actually score worst? A sign error would be invisible to every test in this repository.
3. **Is the spread usable?** If every landscape lands within a few points, players will not see
   their choices reflected, whatever the model does internally.
4. **Is NaN acceptable for `all-nature`, or should it be a number?** See caveat 2.
